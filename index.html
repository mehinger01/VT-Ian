Ian enters junior year with a demanding schedule: Algebra II, Chemistry, Honors History, Honors English, and Engineering. His initial math work shows solid foundational ability. The larger opportunity is helping him consistently complete work, prepare effectively for tests, organize multi-step assignments, and write with enough structure to work independently.

What the current information tells us

Ian does not appear to need broad remediation. He needs a system that protects his grades when the workload becomes complex.

Ability is present

His math assessment was nearly perfect, suggesting the issue is less about basic capability and more about performance under classroom conditions.

Completion must be visible

Missing work hurt his grades last year. Assignments need clear next actions, deadlines, checkpoints, and accountability before they become overdue.

Tests need a process

Math and science assessments were the larger academic weakness. Preparation must include retrieval practice, error analysis, cumulative review, and timed practice.

Session 1: Performance Diagnostic and Immediate Wins

The first session will validate what is already known, identify where performance breaks down, and create systems Ian can use before junior-year coursework accelerates.

0–5

Expectations and roadmap

Set a positive, firm tone and explain that the session will focus on how Ian works—not simply whether he can answer questions.

5–15

Last-year performance review

Map where missing work occurred, how assignments were tracked, when tests became difficult, and what support helped most.

15–27

Math error analysis

Review the one missed assessment item and use several Algebra II readiness problems to distinguish a true gap from a reading, pacing, or process error.

27–39

Science test-preparation challenge

Teach a short retrieval-based study cycle and assess whether Ian can convert notes into questions, explanations, and practice problems.

39–50

Paragraph-by-paragraph writing system

Use a claim–evidence–explanation–connection framework to produce one strong analytical paragraph and show how paragraphs combine into an essay.

50–57

Build the junior-year command center

Create course-specific assignment tracking with due dates, first actions, study dates, and completion checks.

57–60

Commitment and family summary

Identify the highest-value priority, assign one measurable action, and document recommended next steps.

Proposed August Learning Plan

The plan is intentionally adaptive. Session 1 evidence will determine the exact emphasis, but the strongest current priorities are completion, test performance, structured writing, and course-specific readiness.

1

Performance diagnostic and system setup

Confirm how missing work occurred, analyze math errors, test a science study routine, and introduce paragraph-by-paragraph writing.

Outcome: a clear performance profile and one working command center.
2

Completion and workload control

Build daily and weekly routines for tracking assignments across five demanding courses, including recovery steps when work slips.

Outcome: fewer invisible assignments and earlier intervention.
3

Math and chemistry test performance

Practice retrieval, cumulative review, formula selection, error classification, pacing, and checking strategies.

Outcome: a repeatable test-preparation protocol.
4

Honors reading and structured writing

Extract arguments and evidence from history and English readings, then build analytical responses one paragraph at a time.

Outcome: stronger independent writing without waiting for the teacher to supply every step.
5

Engineering projects and integrated planning

Break technical work into milestones, coordinate long-term projects with daily assignments, and document progress.

Outcome: stronger project execution and fewer last-minute surprises.
6+

Twice-weekly academic coaching

One session can emphasize workload, writing, and executive functioning; the second can focus on math, chemistry, and upcoming assessments.

Outcome: consistent support tied directly to live coursework and measurable evidence.
