The Origins of Lacrosse — Part 1

Long before lacrosse became a school or professional sport, Indigenous nations across North America played related stick-and-ball games. Different communities had their own names, rules, field sizes, and purposes. Among the Haudenosaunee, lacrosse has deep cultural and spiritual meaning and is connected to community, identity, and tradition. Early games could involve many more players than modern lacrosse and sometimes stretched across large outdoor areas rather than a standardized field.

These games were not simply recreation. Depending on the community and occasion, they could strengthen relationships, prepare players physically, settle disputes, or serve ceremonial purposes. Because the game existed in several Indigenous cultures before Europeans attempted to standardize it, there was never one single original rulebook.

The Origins of Lacrosse — Part 2

From Indigenous game to codified sport

European observers encountered Indigenous stick-and-ball games centuries ago. During the nineteenth century, non-Indigenous players in Canada increasingly adopted the game and began creating standardized rules. The number of players, field dimensions, equipment, and game structure became more fixed.

This codification helped lacrosse spread as an organized sport, but it also changed how the game's history was often told. Modern versions could be presented as if the sport began when written rules appeared, even though Indigenous communities had played related games for generations. Today, understanding lacrosse history requires recognizing both the development of the modern sport and the much older Indigenous traditions from which it grew.
