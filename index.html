Session 3: The Retention Lab

Goal: retrieve, check, repair, and retrieve again. Answers save automatically in this browser.

1. Baseline: Study your normal way

Instructor: Give Ian three minutes with the mini-lesson below. Do not teach the retention system yet.
MINI-LESSON

Why Objects Move: Newton's Three Laws

First law: An object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity unless an unbalanced force acts on it. This is inertia.

Second law: Acceleration depends on force and mass: F = ma. More force creates more acceleration; more mass requires more force.

Third law: Every force has an equal and opposite force. A rocket pushes exhaust downward, and the exhaust pushes the rocket upward.

Friction opposes motion. Balanced forces do not change motion; unbalanced forces do.

2. Recognition is not recall

Recognition

“That looks familiar when I see it.” Rereading and highlighting often create this feeling.

Recall

“I can produce and use it without looking.” Tests require this.

Key idea: Familiarity can imitate learning. Closed-note retrieval reveals what is actually available.

3. Learn the Retention Cycle

1. LEARNUnderstand the meaning and connect it to prior knowledge.
2. RETRIEVEClose the source and produce the information from memory.
3. CHECK + REPAIRCompare, mark gaps, and correct only what is weak.
4. SPACE + REPEATRetrieve again after time has passed.

4. Choose a retrieval tool

Brain dump

Write everything you know before opening notes.

Teach-back

Explain the idea simply, as though teaching someone younger.

Blank-page reconstruction

Recreate a diagram, formula, sequence, or worked example.

Question conversion

Turn headings and key ideas into questions, then answer closed-note.

5. Retrieval Round 1

Close the mini-lesson and answer without notes.

6. Check and repair

Open the lesson. Compare each answer and classify the result.

Efficiency rule: Do not reread everything. Repair the specific gap, then retrieve it again.

7. Retrieval Round 2

Close the source again. Recreate the lesson in a different form.

8. Math and science transfer

For math

Retrieve the meaning of the formula, when it applies, the steps, a worked example, and common errors. Then solve without the model.

For science

Retrieve vocabulary in your own words, processes, cause and effect, diagrams, comparisons, and applications.

9. Delayed retrieval

Instructor: Complete another activity for 8–10 minutes before returning here.

10. Build the spacing plan

Day 1

Learn → retrieve → check → repair.

Day 2

Five-minute closed-note retrieval.

Day 4

Mixed questions plus application.

Day 7

Final retrieval check.
