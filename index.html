Session 1: Working Diagnostic and Immediate Wins

The first session is intentionally active. Ian will complete authentic tasks, receive direct instruction, and leave with a practical system—not simply answer intake questions.

0–5

Welcome, expectations, and roadmap

Set a positive but firm tone and define expectations for effort, participation, and honest communication.

5–15

Executive-function snapshot

Review planning, organization, task initiation, sustained attention, note-taking, help-seeking, and completion habits.

15–33

Geometry readiness check

Assess essential prerequisites and observe how Ian reads diagrams, organizes steps, responds to challenge, and explains reasoning.

33–45

Science learning and note-taking challenge

Assess comprehension, vocabulary, memory, and note selection, then teach a four-part note-taking structure.

45–55

Build one working system

Create an assignment command center and teach the rule that every assignment needs a visible next action.

55–60

Reflection and next commitment

Identify strengths, priority needs, and one measurable action to complete before the next session.

Proposed August Learning Plan

This arc is flexible. Session 1 evidence and Ian's current coursework will determine exact priorities and pace.

1

Baseline, systems, and immediate wins

Identify executive-function barriers, geometry prerequisites, science-learning habits, and the structure Ian responds to best.

Outcome: a documented learner profile, one working organizational tool, and a clear starting plan.
2

Organization, prioritization, and task initiation

Apply the command center to real schoolwork, break assignments into first actions, estimate time, and practice starting without delay.

Outcome: a realistic workload plan and repeatable start-work routine.
3

Geometry foundations

Target the highest-impact gaps while emphasizing diagrams, multi-step organization, accuracy, and explanation.

Outcome: improved prerequisite fluency and fewer avoidable geometry breakdowns.
4

Science reading, notes, and memory

Use current science material to practice structured notes, vocabulary, retrieval, self-testing, and explanation.

Outcome: a science study routine that turns class material into usable review resources.
5

Integration and independent application

Combine organization, focus cycles, note-taking, and academic strategies using current coursework.

Outcome: evidence of which systems Ian can use independently and where continued coaching offers the greatest return.
