Goal: distinguish familiarity from true readiness, diagnose why mistakes happen, and leave with a test-prep process Ian can actually use.

3. Build Ian's test-prep loop

Learn it→Hide the help→Retrieve→Check→Diagnose→Repair→Retry

Learn it

Understand the concept or procedure first.

Hide the help

Close notes, examples, answer keys, and videos.

Retrieve

Solve, explain, draw, write, or reproduce independently.

Check

Compare the attempt to the correct answer.

Diagnose + repair

Find the exact weakness and fix that weakness, not everything.

Retry

Try again without looking. A correction is not proof until you can reproduce it.

4. Make it fit Ian's classes

Algebra II

Look at an example only if needed → close it → solve a fresh problem → check → identify the exact error → solve another similar problem.

Chemistry

Explain a concept aloud, reconstruct a process or diagram, write definitions from memory, or solve a calculation without following an example.

History

Close the reading and recall the main claim, causes, consequences, people, and evidence.

English

Recall a central idea, evidence, theme, character motivation, or an essay outline without reopening the text first.

5. Diagnose the miss

Getting something wrong is useful only if you know why it went wrong.

Knowledge

I did not know the concept.

Process

I knew the idea but forgot a step.

Mechanics

Calculation, sign, notation, or writing error.

Directions

I misunderstood what the question asked.

Attention

I knew it but lost focus or rushed.

Time pressure

I ran out of time and changed how I worked.

Key idea: “Study more” is not a useful diagnosis. Different errors require different fixes.

6. Green / Yellow / Red readiness

GREEN

I can do or explain this independently.

YELLOW

I partly know it, hesitate, or need a cue.

RED

I cannot produce it without looking.

7. Live pressure test

Tutor: Use real current material if Ian has it. If not, use the prompts below. The goal is the process, not difficult academics.
Math: Solve a short equation or algebra problem without a model. Then classify any miss.
Science: Explain a science concept or process from memory, then check for missing pieces.
Reading: Read a short paragraph once, hide it, then state the central idea, two details, and one unanswered question.

Session 4: Run the School Day

Goal: turn the before / during / after-class framework Ian already knows into an operational system that reliably feeds Todoist and his after-school work.

1. Review the experiment, not the lecture

Tutor: If the experiment worked, move on. If it did not, troubleshoot the failure point rather than reteaching everything.

2. Upgrade Before / During / After

Important: Ian has already learned this framework. Today is about deciding exactly what he does at each stage.

BEFORE: 60-second launch

What are we doing? Is anything due? What do I need open? What am I still confused about?

DURING: capture future Ian

Content, teacher emphasis, actions, and confusion.

AFTER: 2-minute close

What did we learn? What do I have to do? When is it due? Is anything unclear?

3. Before class: make the launch tiny

What class?→Anything due?→What do I need?→What question am I carrying?

4. During class: capture what future Ian needs

Content

Important concept, example, procedure, or model.

Teacher emphasis

“This matters.” “This is on the test.” “Do not forget this.”

Action

Homework, quiz, reading, project step, material to bring.

Confusion

Mark it with a ? so the question survives after class.

Framing: Your notes are not just for the Ian sitting in class. They are for the Ian sitting at home later who no longer remembers what the teacher said.

5. Assignment capture drill

Teacher says: “Finish questions 14–32 tonight. Skip 19 and 27. We’re having a quiz Thursday on sections 2.1 through 2.3. If you’re still confused by factoring, come see me tomorrow.”
Chemistry: “Your lab report is due Monday, but you need your partner’s data by Friday. Your conclusion should explain whether the evidence supported the hypothesis.”

6. Better Todoist entries

Too vague

“Chemistry”

“Study”

“Project”

Visible action

“Complete Chemistry questions 1–10.”

“Retrieve sections 2.1–2.3 without notes.”

“Draft the conclusion paragraph.”

7. After class: the two-minute closeout

What did we learn?→What do I have to do?→When is it due?→What is unclear?→Is another action needed?

8. Simulated school day

Tutor: Give each class one at a time. Ian gets about 60–90 seconds to capture what matters before moving on.
Period 1 — Algebra II: Problems 8–24 odd are due tomorrow. Friday quiz covers the last two lessons. Teacher says completing one mixed-review problem without notes is the best readiness check.
Period 2 — Chemistry: Lab continues tomorrow. Bring goggles. Complete pre-lab questions 1–6. Teacher emphasizes that particle diagrams will appear on the unit test.
Period 3 — English: Read 22 pages tonight. Come ready to discuss how the author develops the main character’s conflict.
Period 4 — History: No homework is announced, but the teacher says the test is next Wednesday and identifies three major causes that students must be able to explain.
Period 5 — Engineering: Your group needs your design sketch finished by Friday so another student can begin the next section.

9. Now you are home

Using only what was captured above, Ian decides what needs attention.

10. Add a disruption

Curveball: You planned to work from 4:00–5:30, but a friend asks you to do something at 5:00. Then you discover you left your chemistry notebook at school.
Goal: Ian should pull from tools he already owns: reprioritize, define the next action, use Todoist, shorten the work block, five-minute start, or Notice → Name → Reset → Resume.
