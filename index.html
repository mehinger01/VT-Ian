The coaching model

Firm expectations, active practice, immediate feedback, and measurable follow-through.

1. See it

Make tasks, deadlines, directions, and next actions visible.

2. Start it

Reduce hesitation by defining the first concrete action and a short focus interval.

3. Finish it

Use checkpoints, quality checks, and accountability to move work across the finish line.

Session 1: Academic Launch Assessment

A high-value working session that combines rapport, diagnostic evidence, direct instruction, and a practical system Ian can use right away.

0–5

Welcome, roadmap, and expectations

Set a positive but firm tone. Ian sees the full plan and understands that effort, explanation, and honest self-assessment matter.

  • Preview the five parts of the session
  • Define what productive struggle looks like
  • Establish a simple redirection cue
5–15

Executive-function snapshot

Use ratings and a realistic workload scenario to observe planning, prioritization, task initiation, and time estimation.

  • Quick self-rating
  • “45 minutes tonight” scenario
  • Compare stated habits with demonstrated choices
15–33

Geometry readiness check

Sample the foundational skills most likely to interfere with geometry while listening closely to Ian's reasoning.

  • Equations, proportions, coordinate plane, angles, area
  • Require selected think-aloud explanations
  • Record whether errors are conceptual, procedural, or attention-related
33–45

Science notes challenge

Observe how Ian identifies, organizes, and remembers information, then teach a four-part note-taking structure.

  • Main idea and essential details
  • Topic → Key idea → Evidence/example → Question
  • Brief retrieval check without looking
45–55

Build Ian's Assignment Command Center

Turn assignments into visible, actionable steps with due dates, estimated time, and help flags.

  • Enter one real or sample assignment
  • Rewrite vague tasks as concrete next actions
  • Practice a 15-minute focus cycle
55–60

Reflection and commitment

Identify what worked, where attention drifted, and one clear action Ian will complete before the next session.

  • Student reflection
  • Tutor summary
  • One measurable accountability commitment

End-of-session deliverable

The minutes tab becomes the parent-facing report: strengths, observed needs, geometry readiness, science-learning observations, response to structure, the tool introduced, and the recommended next session.

Proposed Five-Session Arc

The first session determines the exact emphasis. The arc below shows how twice-weekly coaching could connect habits with real academic growth.

1

Academic Launch Assessment

Executive-function snapshot, geometry readiness, science note-taking, and creation of Ian's Assignment Command Center.

Outcome: a clear learner profile, one working system, and a measurable next step.
2

Plan, Prioritize, and Begin

Use Ian's real workload to practice prioritization, time estimation, task breakdown, and overcoming the “I don't know where to start” barrier.

Outcome: Ian can turn a list of assignments into a realistic work plan with concrete first actions.
3

Geometry Foundations Lab

Teach the highest-priority prerequisite gap identified in Session 1 using modeling, guided practice, error analysis, and independent application.

Outcome: strengthened math foundation plus a repeatable process for reading diagrams and checking work.
4

Science Learning System

Practice strategic reading, structured notes, vocabulary handling, retrieval practice, and quiz preparation using current science content when available.

Outcome: Ian can convert a passage, lecture, or diagram into usable study materials.
5

Independent Performance Challenge

Integrate planning, focus, geometry/science strategy, and self-checking in a realistic academic task with reduced tutor prompting.

Outcome: evidence of what Ian can now do independently and a targeted recommendation for continued support.

What a continuing session looks like

A consistent architecture creates accountability while the academic content changes with Ian's needs.

10 min · Accountability

Review commitments, missing work, wins, barriers, and the current assignment dashboard.

15 min · EF skill

Teach and practice one specific habit such as starting, estimating, prioritizing, note-taking, or checking.

25 min · Academic focus

Geometry or science instruction tied to identified gaps and, whenever possible, Ian's current coursework.

5 min · Independent test

Ian applies the strategy with reduced prompting to show whether the learning transfers.

5 min · Commitment

Define one next action, the evidence of completion, and what will be reviewed next time.

Afterward · Family clarity

Concise notes document progress, needs, completed work, and the next instructional target.
